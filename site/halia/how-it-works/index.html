Experiment two

Then we found the calendar hiding in the grid.

Scroll through the century. Whatever the year, the 1 and the 9 stay lit. Everyone born in the 1900s carries them, so a "missing number" was often just the date system.

We counted every date from 1800 to 2006, 75,605 of them, to measure the bias and correct for it.

What we built

HALIA measures five things.

Each is one plain question answered from 1 to 5, asked again every month. Where you stand today was the strongest signal we found, so that is what HALIA tracks.

Around them sit 24 more questions about your work, money and relationships: 29 in all, one a day, any of them skippable.

What HALIA will not do

  • Predict events, name lucky days or promise outcomes.
  • Tell you whether to marry, stay with or leave someone.
  • Diagnose a health problem or suggest medicines.
  • Recommend investments, loans or trades.

What is not proven yet

  • HALIA shows you no predictions today. The accuracy figures come from research on public data.
  • That its guidance improves lives. That needs a controlled study, which has not been run.
  • That the findings hold in India today. The tests used US earnings data from the 1980s and professional sport.
  • Our first four experiments are being re-run from saved code. The figures from the turn onwards are reproducible now.
